# Breaking News

## Trisolaran Fleet Departs in Historic 1,000-Ship Ceremony

The Trisolaran invasion fleet departed at 0600 stellar time yesterday in a ceremony attended by government officials, military leadership, and an estimated 40 million dehydrated citizens who were rehydrated for the occasion. The fleet, comprising 1,004 vessels, is expected to complete the 450-stellar-cycle journey to the target system without incident, barring chaotic gravitational interference from any wandering stellar bodies encountered en route.

Fleet Commander Ye Gao delivered brief remarks from the command ship's observation deck before leading the assembled officers in three synchronized dehydration salutes. Flags of all eleven Trisolaran provincial confederacies were lowered simultaneously at departure. "We go forth," Commander Gao stated, "so that our civilization may endure." Sun-2 was in stable position at time of departure. Sun-1 and Sun-3 were not.

## Scientists Achieve First Successful 11-Dimensional Proton Fold in Sophon Breakthrough

Physicists at the Trisolaran Academy of Advanced Materials announced the first successful folding of a subatomic proton into 11 dimensions, completing the process required to create a Sophon, after 2,187 failed attempts spanning six stellar cycles. Lead Sophon Engineer Deng Yurun confirmed the achievement in a statement released through the Academy's public affairs office.

"The unfolding into a two-dimensional plane was visible from low orbit for approximately 14 stellar hours," Engineer Deng noted, "which we consider an acceptable duration." The completed Sophon will be dispatched to the target planet, where it is tasked with monitoring surface communications and disrupting high-energy particle accelerator experiments to prevent indigenous scientific advancement. A second Sophon is currently in early-stage dimensional preparation.

## Premature Rehydration Causes Mass Confusion After Chaotic Era Ends Three Minutes Post-Order

Emergency services across the Eastern Continent responded to approximately 2.3 million rehydration injury reports early this morning after an unusually brief Chaotic Era concluded just three minutes following the official dehydration order, leaving citizens in various stages of the rehydration process when stability was declared. Medical teams characterized the incident as the fastest Chaotic Era in recorded history.

"Rapid rehydration presents significant risks of fiber tearing," said Dr. Mao Tian, Director of Emergency Biophysical Services. "Citizens who rehydrated in under four stellar minutes may experience structural discomfort for several cycles." Officials have issued guidance urging affected residents to rehydrate slowly and avoid physical exertion. The government has declined to comment on whether the dehydration order was issued prematurely.
