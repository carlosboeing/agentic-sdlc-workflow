# Chapter 1: First Contact

---

The jump gate spat them out like a bad taste.

Captain Elara Voss gripped the armrest as the *Perseverance* shuddered through the transit corridor, hull plates groaning against forces that physics textbooks never adequately warned you about. The bridge lights flickered amber, red, amber again before settling back to white. Through the forward viewport, the brown dwarf anchoring Station Omega's orbit glowed a sullen crimson against the void.

"Transit complete," Kael Reyes announced from the navigation console, fingers already dancing across the holographic display. "Deviation from projected coordinates: zero point zero three percent. Textbook." They grinned, but Elara caught the way Kael's left hand trembled against their thigh before they tucked it under the console.

"Textbook is what I pay you for, Lieutenant." Elara released the armrest. "Status report."

"Engineering nominal," Ren Takahashi's voice crackled from two decks below. "ARIA, how are we feeling? ... She says the port stabilizer coupling absorbed more stress than expected. I will run a diagnostic before we jump again." A pause. "Forty-eight hours on the cooldown clock, Captain. We are grounded at Omega until then."

Forty-eight hours. The rule every pilot learned on day one. Jump gates fold space, but the energy leaves the quantum anchors destabilized for two days. No shortcuts, no overrides. If something went wrong here, retreat was not an option until the gate cooled.

Elara crossed to the science station, where Dr. Maren Osei sat with her back perfectly straight and her hands folded in her lap.

"Doctor. Anything from the arrays?"

Maren's eyes did not leave the screen. "Background noise. Nothing anomalous yet." Her voice was level — the voice that had earned her the callsign Whisper. But Elara noticed the contingency binders on the shelf behind her, reorganized since their last conversation. Maren had spent her career preparing for contact, and now that they were one jump from the Anomaly, she looked like someone standing at a cliff's edge.

"Keep scanning. I want a full analysis before we—"

ARIA interrupted. The ship's AI rarely spoke unbidden, and when she did, the bridge went quiet.

"Captain Voss. I am detecting a structured electromagnetic emission from bearing two-seven-one mark fifteen. The signal does not match any registered communication protocol in my database."

The bridge went still.

Maren's hands unfolded. She turned to her console with a speed that betrayed everything her calm voice tried to conceal. "Frequency?"

"Repeating pattern at 73-minute intervals," ARIA said. "The modulation is non-random. I assess with high confidence that the signal is artificial in origin."

The word hung in the recycled air. *Artificial.* Three centuries of searching, and it arrived not as a broadcast to Earth but as a quiet observation from a ship's AI at the edge of charted space.

Kael turned in their chair. "Captain, that bearing is beyond the twelfth gate — the uncharted one the Accord activated three months ago. One jump to investigate. But nobody has gone through that gate and come back."

"Which is precisely why we should proceed with caution," Ren cut in. "ARIA and I can run a full signal decomposition in twelve hours. No reason to rush toward something we do not understand."

But Elara heard the current beneath the caution. Ren was already thinking about what that signal might teach them. The engineer who talked to the ship's AI like a friend was not afraid of the unknown — they were afraid of missing it.

Elara looked at Maren. The xenobiologist had gone pale.

"Doctor Osei. Your assessment."

Maren took a breath. When she spoke, her voice was barely above a whisper. "We have spent three centuries listening to the stars. What if they have been listening back?"

Elara felt the decision settling onto her shoulders. Investigate and risk the crew in uncharted space. Retreat and spend a lifetime wondering.

She opened her mouth to speak.

ARIA spoke first.

"Captain. The signal has changed."

Every head turned.

"Clarify."

"The 73-minute interval has collapsed to continuous transmission. The modulation pattern has shifted — it is no longer repeating." A pause, brief and calculated. "The new pattern correlates with our arrival signature. The signal is responding to us."

Kael's hand started trembling again.
