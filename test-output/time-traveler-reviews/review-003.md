# Runnymede Meadow — Signing of the Magna Carta

**Rating:** ★★★★☆
**Reviewer:** HistoryHopper_Prime
**Date Visited:** 15 June 1215
**Date Reviewed:** 15 March 2026
**Trip Type:** School Group

---

## Review

Brought fourteen teenagers here for an "immersive history experience." The meadow is genuinely lovely — rolling grass, the Thames glinting in the distance, pleasant summer air. Four stars for the scenery. Everything else requires a frank conversation.

The ceremony was, as my most articulate student put it, "basically just some guys arguing and then signing a piece of paper." There were barons — surprisingly well-dressed, very sharp tunics — and one visibly annoyed king who looked like he'd rather be anywhere else. The actual signing took about forty-five seconds. Several students were audibly on their phones before the ink dried.

Marketing: nonexistent. No banners. No merchandise. Not a single commemorative parchment for sale — at a *parchment signing event*. A gift shop would not have gone amiss.

Catering: there was none. I asked. A squire looked at me as though I had requested calculus. Thirteen hungry teenagers in a field is not a situation I recommend.

No toilet facilities either, which is perhaps unsurprising for 1215 but still worth noting in a review.

Still — this is the founding document of constitutional liberty. Worth visiting. Pack lunch.
