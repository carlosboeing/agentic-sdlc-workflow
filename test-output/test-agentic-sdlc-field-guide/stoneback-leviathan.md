# Stoneback Leviathan (*Megabesta lithodus*)

## Classification

**Phylum:** Megafauna Mythica
**Family:** Lithobetidae
**Genus:** *Megabesta*
**Species:** *M. lithodus*

## Physical Description

The Stoneback Leviathan is a colossal aquatic creature rivalling small islands in mass. Its dorsal surface is encrusted with centuries of sediment, barnacles, and mineral deposits — a stone carapace indistinguishable from a rocky shoal. Specimens measure 60-80 metres in length with a serpentine body and broad paddle-like flukes. The pale underside pulses with bioluminescence. Four lateral fins drive it through ocean trenches with deceptive speed.

## Habitat

Stoneback Leviathans inhabit the deepest oceanic basins, surfacing only during migratory seasons or when drawn upward by geological disturbance. They favour tectonic shelf edges and hydrothermal vent fields, where mineral-rich currents sustain their unusual biology. Sightings correlate strongly with seismic activity: tremors appear to rouse the creatures from prolonged dormancy.

## Diet

These behemoths sustain themselves through filter-feeding on deep-sea biomass and by drawing minerals through their skin — a process expedition reports term "lithic absorption." They also consume large cephalopods and whale carcasses opportunistically when passive nutrition is insufficient.

## Behaviour

Stoneback Leviathans are largely passive and solitary. Their characteristic behaviour is extended stillness — individuals may remain motionless on the sea floor for years, so thoroughly camouflaged that ships have anchored to their backs in error. When disturbed, they surface slowly, creating hazardous swells before submerging again without any apparent aggression. Vocalizations consist of infrasonic resonance detectable hundreds of kilometres away, likely used for long-range mate location.

## Threat Level

**2 out of 5** — Stoneback Leviathans pose near-zero deliberate threat to humanoids. Casualties arise exclusively from passive hazards: vessels running aground on their backs, or displacement waves generated during surfacing events. Their immense mass renders them impervious to conventional deterrents, so avoidance remains the recommended field protocol.

## Interspecies Relationships

The Stoneback Leviathan's niche intersects with several catalogued species. Emberwing Drakes roost on surfaced Leviathans during oceanic migrations, apparently mistaking the mineral-encrusted dorsal ridge for volcanic terrain — the Leviathan appears indifferent to this. The relationship with the Mirrorghast is more contested: expedition reports from 1966 describe spectral entities clustering near the bioluminescent underside at depth, drawn by concentrated light in an otherwise lightless environment, much as they pursue lanterns in cave systems.
