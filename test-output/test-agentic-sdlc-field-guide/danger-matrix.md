# Thornwood Danger Classification Matrix

*Emberveil Highlands Expedition — Risk Assessment*

Axes rated 1–5; Predictability: 1 = unpredictable. **Encounter Risk** = sum of four axes — Low (4–8), Moderate (9–13), High (14–17), Extreme (18–20).

## Emberwing Drake (*Draconis ignis*)

| Axis | Score | Justification |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Aggression | 3 | Profile rates threat level 3/5; minimal aggression unless nesting sites are threatened. |
| Evasion Difficulty | 4 | Soaring on thermals at six hundred metres, the drake controls all engagement range. |
| Environmental Hazard | 4 | Day 12 camp sat amid sulphurous air, scorched basalt, and shed obsidian moult-fragments. |
| Predictability | 4 | Territorial thresholds are well-defined; expedition precautions worked because the pattern held. |

**Encounter Risk: 15 — High**

## Mirrorghast (*Phantasma specularis*)

| Axis | Score | Justification |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Aggression | 1 | Profile confirms no direct aggression; on Day 13 the entity ceased pursuit once lights were extinguished. |
| Evasion Difficulty | 3 | Retreat by touch through absolute darkness consumed forty minutes, negating the party's mobility. |
| Environmental Hazard | 4 | Profile warns the Mirrorghast lures travelers into hazardous cave terrain rather than confronting them directly. |
| Predictability | 2 | Attraction to light is documented, but the profile acknowledges intentions remain poorly understood. |

## Stoneback Leviathan (*Megabesta lithodus*)

| Axis | Score | Justification |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Aggression | 2 | Day 14 records the leviathan moving with unhurried patience, showing no awareness of the expedition. |
| Evasion Difficulty | 2 | Its slow, continental movement gives a withdrawing party substantial time advantage across open terrain. |
| Environmental Hazard | 5 | The dorsal ridge cleared the tallest pines; ground compression and toppled timber are unavoidable nearby. |
| Predictability | 3 | Bearings and travel direction were recorded reliably on Day 14, though sample data remains extremely limited. |

## Reviewer's Note

*Dr. Seraphina Coldwell, Chair of Field Risk Assessment, Royal Society of Cryptozoological Studies*

I formally dispute the Emberwing Drake's Aggression rating of 3. Thornwood's Day 12 notes document flame-burst vocalizations and claw impressions from repeated ground-level approaches — not passive behaviour by any interpretation. A rating of 4 is more defensible, and field parties should plan accordingly rather than rely on the lenient figure given here.
