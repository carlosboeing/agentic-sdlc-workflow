# Survival Protocols for Cryptozoological Field Work

*Compiled by Professor Aldric Thornwood, Fellow of the Royal Society of Cryptozoological Studies*

The Emberveil Highlands expedition demonstrated how rapidly theoretical knowledge collapses under field conditions. What follows are survival protocols distilled from direct encounters with three classified species. Every recommendation was earned through proximity to creatures that could have ended the expedition permanently.

---

## Protocol Alpha: Emberwing Drake (*Draconis ignis*)

**Danger Classification: High (Encounter Risk 15)**

### If You Encounter an Emberwing Drake

1. Extinguish all flames and heat-emitting equipment immediately. Drakes interpret thermal signatures within their caldera range as rival encroachment.
2. Descend to the nearest ravine or depression. The drake controls engagement range from altitude, and elevated positions leave you exposed to diving passes.
3. Remain stationary and cover exposed skin with damp cloth to reduce your thermal profile.
4. Do not withdraw until the drake has returned to soaring altitude for a minimum of thirty minutes. As documented during the Day 12 encounter, the drake circled overhead for hours after detecting our camp — premature movement would have drawn a descent.

### Warning Signs

- Scorched basalt in concentric arcs, indicating flame-burst activity near a defended perimeter
- Shed obsidian plating or claw impressions exceeding thirty centimetres
- Sulphurous air intensifying beyond ambient volcanic levels

### Equipment Required

- Thermal suppression blankets (aluminium-lined, two per party member)
- Sealed lamp housings with instant-shutoff valves
- Fireproof cases for field notes and specimens

## Protocol Beta: Stoneback Leviathan (*Megabesta lithodus*)

**Danger Classification: Moderate (Encounter Risk 12)**

### If You Encounter a Stoneback Leviathan

1. Establish distance immediately. The leviathan's slow gait is deceptive — ground compression and falling timber create a hazard radius well beyond the creature itself.
2. Move perpendicular to the creature's line of travel, never parallel. Its destruction corridor extends far in the direction of movement.
3. Gain high ground where possible. Elevated rock formations resist the ground compression that collapses soil at lower elevations.

### Warning Signs

- Trees leaning uniformly in one direction, indicating recent passage
- Ground tremors detectable underfoot at distances exceeding two kilometres

### Equipment Required

- Field binoculars rated for four-kilometre observation range
- Topographical maps marking ridgelines and stable rock for retreat
- Vibration-sensing stakes for camp perimeter early warning

## Protocol Gamma: Mirrorghast (*Phantasma specularis*)

**Danger Classification: Moderate (Encounter Risk 10)**

### If You Encounter a Mirrorghast

1. Extinguish all light sources without hesitation. The Mirrorghast advances toward any active illumination.
2. Retreat by touch along the cave wall, maintaining physical contact with the person ahead. As documented during the Day 13 incident, the expedition retreated through absolute darkness for forty minutes using this method.
3. Do not re-ignite any light until you reach natural daylight. The creature ceased pursuit at the cave mouth only because no further light was presented.

### Warning Signs

- Prismatic reflections from surfaces that are not mineral formations
- Ambient luminescence in cave galleries with no geological explanation

### Equipment Required

- Tactile guideline rope with knotted distance markers for lightless navigation
- Shielded lanterns with covered shutters for instant blackout
- Crystallographic reagent for post-encounter spectral analysis

## General Field Safety

1. No specimen is worth a life. If the danger matrix rates a creature High or above, observation distance must exceed one kilometre.
2. Every party member must know the relevant creature protocol before entering that species' range. Briefings are mandatory.
3. Maintain a supply reserve of two days beyond the planned expedition duration. Depleted supplies force rushed decisions, as the Thornwood expedition learned on Day 14.
4. All light and heat sources must be controllable to full blackout within five seconds. This single capability saved the expedition in the selenite caverns.
5. When in doubt, withdraw. Record observations from a safe distance and return better equipped. The creatures will still be there.

---

## Author's Note

Science demands proximity, but survival demands restraint. These protocols represent the narrow margin between the two, refined through encounters that could easily have ended otherwise. I trust future expeditions will find them useful — and return safely to improve upon them.
