## Featured Destinations

### The Great Fire of London (2–5 September 1666)

An immersive urban experience. Guests describe the aroma — burning timber, melting tallow, and every bakery in the city combusting simultaneously — as "indescribable." Evening views of flames reflected across the Thames are a visual highlight. Fire safety infrastructure is pre-modern: evacuation support consists of a bucket and directions to the river.

### Giza Plateau — Great Pyramid Construction (c. 2560 BCE)

Witness 2.3 million limestone blocks assembled with impossibly tight joints. The engineering is genuinely breathtaking. Amenities, however, are nonexistent — no gift shop, no audio guide, no shade structures. Accommodation is communal reed mats. Cuisine: bread, onions, and fermented fish sauce.

### Runnymede Meadow — Signing of the Magna Carta (15 June 1215)

A scenic meadow ideal for constitutional history enthusiasts. The signing ceremony lasts approximately forty-five seconds. Catering is nonexistent — one traveler asked a squire, who looked at them as though they had requested calculus. No merchandise available, a remarkable oversight at a parchment signing event.

### Sea of Tranquility — Apollo 11 Moon Landing (20 July 1969)

Our highest-rated destination. Guests report weeping inside their helmets at the sight of Earth in a black sky. After the first hour of low-gravity bouncing, however, there is literally nothing to do. No restaurants, no nightlife, and not a single bar of cell service.
