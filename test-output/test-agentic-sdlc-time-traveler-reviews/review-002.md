## Review

Booked this as a romantic getaway under the listing title "Wonder of the World — All-Inclusive Experience." Spoiler: do NOT trust that description.

Yes, the scale is genuinely breathtaking. Watching 2.3 million limestone blocks hauled across the Giza Plateau by tens of thousands of workers is objectively one of the most impressive things I have ever witnessed across any timeline. The precision engineering alone deserves recognition — those joints are impossibly tight. Credit where it is due.

That said, the amenities are nonexistent. No gift shop, no audio guide, no shade structures — direct Egyptian sun at peak summer with zero relief. My partner got a sunburn so severe she spent our second day in the medical tent, which was also not air-conditioned.

The "all-inclusive resort" listed in the package was a worker's camp. Communal sleeping on reed mats. Food was bread, onions, and fermented fish sauce. Construction noise starts at dawn — the moment Ra crests the horizon, the hammering begins.

Sand everywhere. In everything. My camera bags took three cleanings.

Three stars for the engineering. Two docked for the worst visitor experience in recorded history — and I have visited a lot of history.
