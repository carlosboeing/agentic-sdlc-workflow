## Purpose

This memo proposes amendments to the MFSA allergen disclosure framework. Current standards assume stable molecular composition, constant patron biology, and uniform translation of "allergy" across dimensions — assumptions this cycle's inspections have disproved.

## Current Regulatory Framework

Directive 22-A requires allergen posting using fixed molecular identifiers, designed for single-dimension establishments where ingredients maintain stable state. It contains no provisions for food in superposition, ingredients changing composition across dimensional boundaries, or dishes that retroactively cease to exist — rendering it unenforceable for cross-dimensional establishments.

## Evidence from Recent Inspections

MFSA-INS-2026-001 (The Quantum Bistro) documented food in unresolved cooked/raw superposition — a dish containing peanut protein in one collapsed state may contain none in another, making fixed allergen labels meaningless.

MFSA-INS-2026-003 (The Nth Dimension Noodle House) found noodle filaments breaching the N/N+1 boundary and portion sizes shifting per diner's dimensional state. A wheat-based ingredient at N=7 may assume unclassified molecular structure at N=8, invalidating allergen labels each time N increments.

MFSA-INS-2026-004 (The Probability Cafe) revealed dishes retroactively ceasing to exist post-consumption. Patrons suffer allergic reactions to food that never existed by the time treatment arrives, and the framework offers no guidance when the trigger has been ontologically annulled.

MFSA-INS-2026-007 (The Entropy Tavern) documented meals un-cooking within a reverse-entropy pocket, with a cooked egg reverting to ambulatory raw form — reintroducing albumin sensitivity risks cooking had neutralized.

## Proposed Amendments

**Amendment 1 — Probabilistic Allergen Labeling (Section 3):** Establishments serving superposition food must disclose allergen content as a probability matrix with manifestation percentages. Rationale: binary present/absent labels are meaningless for quantum-state cuisine.

**Amendment 2 — Dimensional-Transition Recertification (Section 7, new):** Ingredients crossing a dimensional boundary must undergo allergen recertification at the destination. Rationale: molecular composition does not persist across transitions.

**Amendment 3 — Retroactive Allergen Liability (Section 12, new):** Liability attaches at consumption and is not extinguished by ontological annulment of the dish. Rationale: allergic reactions persist even when the cause does not.

**Amendment 4 — Temporal-Reversion Protocols (Section 14, new):** Establishments in entropy-reversal zones must disclose allergen profiles for all intermediate states a dish may assume during reversion. Rationale: un-cooking reintroduces allergen risks cooking had eliminated.

## Impact Assessment

**Single-dimension establishments** face minimal burden; Amendments 2-4 do not apply, and Amendment 1 affects only those using probability-field cooking.

**Cross-dimensional establishments** bear the greatest cost, requiring real-time allergen monitoring across transitions. A two-cycle phase-in with subsidized equipment grants is proposed.

**Void-based establishments** pose a unique challenge — Dimension 0-Null ingredients may lack molecular structure entirely, requiring provisional exemption pending a non-molecular allergen taxonomy.

## Stakeholder Consultation Summary

**Restaurant Owners' Guild:** Supported the phase-in but requested three cycles. Opposed probabilistic labeling as "confusing to patrons who prefer certainty about what will kill them."

**MFSA Inspector Corps:** Endorsed all amendments, noting current enforcement is impossible for cross-dimensional allergens and any structured framework improves operations.

**Diner Advocacy Coalition:** Strongly supported all amendments, citing pending claims from dishes that retroactively ceased to exist. Called the framework "a bureaucratic void that real people fall through."

## Recommendation

This Department recommends immediate adoption. Phase one (Amendments 1 and 3, within 60 days) addresses probabilistic labeling and retroactive liability, requiring no new infrastructure. Phase two (Amendments 2 and 4, within 120 days) allows procurement of monitoring equipment and temporal-reversion training.
