# Field Notes: The Thornwood Expedition, Day 12-14

*Professor Aldric Thornwood, Fellow of the Royal Society of Cryptozoological Studies*
*Location: The Emberveil Highlands, Eastern Ranges*

---

## Day 12 — Arrival at the Caldera Basin

The ascent from Base Camp Aurelius took eleven hours. I write this by lamplight in a shallow cave overlooking the caldera floor, the air thick with the sulphurous tang of volcanic exhalation. The signs are unmistakable: scorched basalt in concentric arcs, claw impressions measuring forty centimetres across, and fragments of obsidian plating shed in what I judge to be a recent moult. We are camped within the established territory of an Emberwing Drake (*Draconis ignis*). Ferris spotted wing-shadow at dusk — a large specimen, banking on a thermal some six hundred metres overhead. We kept the lamps dimmed and made no fire. The drake did not descend.

---

## Day 13 — The Cave of Refractions

This morning we entered the selenite cavern system marked on Hargrove's 1887 survey. We should not have brought lanterns so deep. At the third gallery, Pemberton's torch caught a surface that was not rock — luminous, shifting, a prismatic entity two metres tall that regarded us with what I can only describe as curiosity. A Mirrorghast (*Phantasma specularis*), unmistakably. It began advancing, drawn to our light. I ordered all lamps extinguished immediately. In absolute darkness we retreated by touch alone, Pemberton's hand on my coat-tail. When we emerged into daylight forty minutes later and looked back, the gallery glowed faintly — the creature had ceased pursuit at the cave mouth. A narrow escape, and an instructive one.

---

## Day 14 — Retreat from the Ridge

Dawn brought fog, but the ridge was clear by midmorning. Pemberton saw it first and said nothing — simply pointed toward the northern watershed. At a distance I estimate at four kilometres, moving through the treeline with the unhurried patience of continental drift, was a Stoneback Leviathan (*Megabesta lithodus*). The dorsal ridge alone cleared the tallest pines. I recorded bearings, a rough silhouette sketch, and the direction of travel. Then I gave the order to withdraw. No scientific objective is worth the risk of a closer approach with our depleted supplies and two porters already nursing injuries from yesterday. We begin the descent at first light tomorrow.
