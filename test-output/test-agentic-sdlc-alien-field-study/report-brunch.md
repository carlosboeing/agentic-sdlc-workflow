# Field Report #7741-B: The Cult of Late-Morning Dining

**Researcher:** Zlyx-9 Oorvenath, Senior Anthropological Observer
**Department:** Terrestrial Behavioural Studies, Galactic Institute of Sapient Curiosity

## Observation

The bipeds gather between 10:00 and 14:00 on designated rest days to perform a ritual designated "brunch." Subjects routinely wait 90 minutes for table allocation, exhibiting no visible distress.[^1] Upon seating, *Homo sapiens* consume a fermented citrus-ethanol beverage in quantities inconsistent with mere hydration. A mashed green paste spread upon toasted grain fragments is photographed extensively before consumption is permitted.[^2] The bipeds appear to derive meaning from the delay itself.

## Cultural Analysis

Brunch appears to function as a quasi-religious observance, structuring the weekend around a shared act of consumption. The ritual serves clear status-signalling purposes: venue selection and beverage choice are subject to rigorous peer scrutiny. Paradoxically, the bipeds pay premium prices for foodstuffs available at any hour in their domestic environments, suggesting the transaction carries symbolic rather than nutritional significance.

## Recommendations

Longitudinal observation is warranted to determine whether the 90-minute wait constitutes willing sacrifice or inadvertent conditioning. A dedicated study of "bottomless mimosa" dynamics is proposed for the subsequent reporting cycle.
